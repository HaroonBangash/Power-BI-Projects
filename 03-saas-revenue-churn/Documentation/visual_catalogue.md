# Visual Catalogue - SaaS Revenue, Retention & Churn

shows. Generated by `Python/04_generate_report.py`; every figure quoted here was read
off a rendered capture in `Validation/evidence/`, not typed from memory.

## Theme — ink on paper

Taken from the client's reference design: a **light** report drawn in two tones.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ground** | Soft pink `#F7E4E8`, with every card white and edged in a hairline `#E4D6D9` |
| **Rail** | One solid crimson column `#A4132C`, full height, drawn as a chromeless textbox at `z = 0` so the buttons stand on it |
| **Data** | Crimson `#9E1B32` and pink `#F2A0AC`, with rose `#E0788C` between them. Won is the light tone, lost is the dark one |
| **Supporting** | Plum, terracotta, slate, teal, mauve, olive — muted, and used **only** where a chart has more categories than two tones can separate (a six-slice pie, a seven-channel treemap) |

**The cards do not rotate through a spectrum.** The reference highlights ONE card and
leaves the rest plain, which is a quieter hierarchy than eight hues in a row — so the
first card in every row is the **hero**, filled in that page's own light tone with a
deep-crimson figure on it, and the supporting cards are white with a crimson figure.
Eight pages, eight hero tones: pink, apricot, mauve, salmon, lilac, sand, rose, pale
slate.

**The buttons are deliberately unlike the other projects in this series**, which all
used filled slate rectangles on a dark rail. Here the rail *is* the colour: the page you
are on is a solid white block with crimson text, and every other page is the rail itself
with a hairline outline.

Registered as a custom theme
(`StaticResources/RegisteredResources/NorthstarCrimson.json`) plus matching explicit
colours in the generator, because a visual's own formatting overrides the theme.
`Validation/validate_report_layout.py` checks it on every page — that the rail carries
only white or crimson buttons, that exactly one card per page is filled in that page's
tone, and that every other card is white with a crimson figure.

Two charts colour themselves from their data: the churn-driver bars (anything inside
|r| < 0.05 is drawn flat grey, because it is not a driver — direction is read off the
axis) and the cohort matrix (a four-step pink ramp, every step light enough to carry
dark text, blank where the cohort has not lived that long).

**One thing inverts on a light ground.** The dead-zone trick that makes a matrix
readable — paint the insignificant cells the *panel* colour so they disappear — means
white here, not charcoal. Ported unchanged from a dark theme it would have painted every
quiet cell in a dark block, which is the opposite of what it is for.

## Review: what the renders and the domain review found

Every one of these passed the schema checks, the field checks and the query checks. They
are only visible once the thing is drawn.

| # | Found | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ***Model:* "support contact rate by plan" read 1.68 for Enterprise against 0.43 for Starter** — four times the contacts from the customers who churn least. `DimPlan` reached only the three subscription-shaped facts, so a plan filter shrank the DENOMINATOR (months of customer life) while the NUMERATOR (all 45,000 tickets, which hang off `DimCustomer`) stayed whole | Plan moved onto `DimCustomer` and filters through the customer. Sound because this source gives every customer one subscription for life, on one plan — now checked in `09_validation.sql` |
| 2 | *Model:* `VAR Group` and `VAR Before` are reserved DAX names; either makes the whole model script fail with "Failed to resolve name 'SYNTAXERROR'", and Power BI then strips the measure from visuals | Renamed; both added to the verifier's reserved-word list so the next one fails statically |
| 3 | *Model:* churn and retention returned a value for September 2026, a month the data does not reach — 0% churn and 100% retention out of pure absence of data | Every ratio now requires the month itself to be inside the data |
| 4 | *Model:* a churn rate of nought rendered BLANK, because `DIVIDE ( BLANK, n )` is BLANK — a month where nobody left left a GAP in the line rather than sitting on the axis | `COALESCE` the numerator |
| 5 | *Model:* a measure reference inside a `CALCULATE` boolean filter fails with "a function 'PLACEHOLDER' has been used in a True/False expression" | Hoisted into a `VAR` first |
| 6 | Four retention cards and a multi-row card rendered blank: a retention rate has no meaning over all time, because there is no month before the first one | Explicit latest-month measures. A visual-level date filter would have been shorter and wrong — the active date relationship on `FactSubscription` is its START date, so it would have redefined "median tenure" as "of customers who signed up in August 2026" |
| 7 | The waterfall drew two bars of five: the three impossible movements were blank, so they vanished — defeating the point of showing them | A measure that returns 0 rather than blank, so all five appear on the axis |
| 8 | The cohort matrix scrolled in both directions at 64% width | Full width; 2025 cohorts, M0–M12 |
| 9 | Eleven driver bars in a 244 px row showed seven | The evidence chart takes a full-height column and the page stacks beside it |
| 10 | The field-parameter chart showed six zeros — with nothing selected it groups by every parameter row, and each one equals the overall rate | A default selection on the slicer, which the reader can still change |
| 11 | A `scatterChart` of four plans is four bubbles | Plan × segment, twelve bubbles, sized by customers |
| 12 | Small multiples stacked 3 × 1 lost their panel titles at ~80 px | 1 × 3 |
| 13 | A table of eleven correlations totalled them | Totals off |
| 14 | The multi-row card and the movement table both truncated their text | Shorter labels; the table widened and its source text shortened |

## Visual types considered and rejected

- **The `kpi` visual.** Below goal it fills its whole panel with the goal-distance colour
  and buries its own number on a dark canvas (found in an earlier project; not retried).
- **`ribbonChart`.** Tried in the lab: four plans whose ranks never change is a flat
  ribbon, and the reader learns nothing from it.
- **A funnel.** There is no funnel in this data — no leads, no trials, no stages. Drawing
  one would be invention.
- **A churn-risk gauge or health score.** The evidence on page 4 is that product usage
  does not predict churn here. A health score built on it would be decoration with a
  number on it.
