# Visual Catalogue

Eight pages, **218 visuals**, **22 visual types**, generated by
`Python/04_generate_report.py`. Nothing is authored by clicking in Power BI Desktop.

Every visual carries a `demonstrates` note in `Validation/report_manifest.json` saying
what it is there for — which is also how a chart that has no reason to exist gets
caught before it is drawn.

---

## Navigation — a top bar, not a rail

Every other project in this series put a column of buttons down the left-hand side.
This one runs a row of tabs across the top. The page you are on is a filled block with
mint bold text and a mint underline beneath it; every other tab is bare text on the
bar. It is a different device in a different place — and it hands 168 px of width back
to the content, which a 47-month receivable chart uses.

Two things about the bar are worth recording, because both cost a render to find:

- **A rectangle has to be an action button, not a textbox.** An empty textbox is forced
  to a minimum height of about 24 px, so the 3 px tab indicator rendered as a mint blob.
- **A textbox draws a text caret** at the end of its content in Desktop, and it lands in
  every capture. The brand wordmark is a button with its text on and everything else
  off, for that reason alone.

## Theme — a dark operations console

A revenue cycle is an operational discipline: a denials queue, an ageing ladder, a
clock. So the report is built like a console rather than a board pack.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ground** | Deep teal `#0A1D22`, with the bar above it darker still at `#071619` |
| **Panels** | Lifted teal `#10292F` on a hairline `#1C3D45` border |
| **Accent** | Mint `#37C9A8`, and it means *"this is the thing"* |
| **Ageing ramp** | Mint → teal → sky → amber → orange → coral, cool to hot |
| **Supporting** | Sky, amber, coral, violet, lime — used **only** where a chart has more categories than the accent and one muted tone can separate |

**The discipline is that colour is not decoration.** The ageing ramp is the *only*
ordered colour scale anywhere in the report, because age is the only quantity here that
genuinely has an order. Everything else is either the accent or a muted tone. Three
charts colour themselves from their own data:

- the **signal chart** — a dimension that passed the stated test is drawn in the accent,
  one that failed is drawn flat grey, so the page says *"nothing here"* with its ink as
  well as its words;
- the **payer chart** — Self Pay in the accent, the five insurers in one muted tone,
  because the finding is the split between them and not the differences among the five;
- the **ageing ladder** — the ramp above.

**Hierarchy is carried by elevation, not hue.** On a dark ground, filling a card with a
colour buries its own number — the first thing a render showed. So the hero card on each
row is lifted one shade (`#143840`) and writes its figure in mint; the rest sit on the
panel colour in ink.

Registered as a custom theme (`StaticResources/RegisteredResources/NorthstarConsole.json`)
*plus* matching explicit colours in the generator, because a visual's own formatting
overrides the theme.

## The pages

### 1 · Executive Summary — what we billed, what came in, what is owed
Six cards, a three-line funnel by month, a donut of the four buckets the allowed amount
splits into, monthly volume, status mix, and the three collection rates on one
multi-row card. The note says why the net rate appears twice.

### 2 · Revenue Cycle — the money chain
A **funnel** for billed → allowed → collected, a 100% stacked column showing what
happened to each month's allowed amount, allowed and collected by payer, net collection
by facility type, and a **gauge** against a stated 95% benchmark.

### 3 · Accounts Receivable — how much, how old, what moved it
An **area chart** of open AR at each month end since January 2023 — it never turns — a
**waterfall** of the four movements, the ageing ladder in the ramp, and a payer × age
matrix. The note explains why this ageing is arithmetic and not a collections story.

### 4 · Denials — what, why, and by whom
Denials by reason, a **treemap** of denied value by category and reason, denial rate by
payer coloured by the one real split, the queue by workflow state, and the rate by
adjudication month. The note states plainly that no denial in this file ever recovers.

### 5 · What Predicts a Denial — the brief's question, answered honestly
Cramér's V for all ten candidate dimensions, the distribution of 500 providers' denial
rates (a textbook bell curve: 6, 83, 189, 156, 56, 9, 1), a **funnel plot** of every
provider against its own error bar, and the test table with verdicts.

### 6 · Timeliness — the part of the cycle that works
Three intervals on one axis, volume against speed, days to cash by payer and by
remittance method, and a gauge on the provider's own submission discipline.

### 7 · Service & Patient Mix — volume only
A **decomposition tree** over five attributes, a treemap of procedure volume by service
line, ICD-10 categories (all ten identical at 25,000 lines), and risk band within age
band. No page ranks a procedure by money, and the note says why.

### 8 · Data & Method — what was assumed, tested and refused
The audit findings recomputed on every refresh, the assumptions, the limits, and the
traps in this source. Two identity gaps on cards, both reading zero.

## Nine defects the renders found that no query check would have

A visual can return perfectly correct data and still mislead. These were all caught by
opening the page and looking at it.

1. **A 44-month categorical axis crowded every label into `Jan 20..` and then scrolled.**
   `MonthStart` is a date column, so the axis is continuous and ticks by year instead.

2. **Three "clocks" were plotted on two different calendars.** Days to submit and days
   to adjudicate read `FactClaim`, whose active date is the SERVICE date; days to cash
   read `FactPayment`, whose active date is the PAYMENT date. On one axis that put
   service months and payment months together, and the line ran off the top of the chart
   in the last two months — when cash was still arriving for care delivered earlier. A
   claim-basis version of days-to-cash fixed it; the two agree exactly at the grand
   total, because they average the same 69,111 lags.

3. **A bar chart with ten categories in a 228 px panel shows six and hides four behind a
   scrollbar** — and on the evidence page the four it hid were the dimensions that
   FAILED the test, which is the entire point of the page. Columns instead of bars.

4. **A table cannot drop its total row**, and the audit-findings table was summing
   counts, dollar amounts and percentages into `128,912.5482`. Both reference tables are
   matrices with subtotals off, with the text column rendered as a text *measure*.

5. **A gauge with no maximum scales to twice its own value**, which puts any number in
   the middle of the dial and says nothing. Both gauges now carry an explicit maximum.

6. **A decomposition tree at 180 px drew one level and nothing else.** It moved to the
   228 px row.

7. **The two freshest ageing buckets vanished from the ladder** — they are empty, and a
   blank bar silently disappears. `+ 0` makes them draw at zero, which is the thing
   worth seeing: nothing has been submitted for 84 days.

8. **An empty textbox is forced to ~24 px**, so a 3 px tab indicator rendered as a blob.

9. **A textbox draws a text caret in Desktop** and it appears in every capture.

## The rules the pages were built to

- Money axes start at zero.
- A single-measure bar or column chart ignores a measure-keyed `dataPoint.fill` and
  falls back to the palette's first hue; `defaultColor` is what it actually reads.
- A slicer cannot hold a default selection, and a filter *on* a slicer restricts its
  items rather than selecting one — so every page is designed so its unselected state
  is true.
- Gauge ≥ 110 px or the arc clips; multi-row card ≥ 110 px for three rows; a matrix
  ≥ 180 px; a decomposition tree ≥ 228 px and about four bars a level.
- Every page's footer note states the window the page is measured over and the one
  thing a reader would otherwise get wrong.
