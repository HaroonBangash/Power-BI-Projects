## Three decisions that shape everything else

### 1. Every fact carries its own dimension keys

A filter never travels from one fact table to another. If the payer, facility, provider
and patient keys lived only on the claim header, then *"line volume by payer"* or
*"denials by specialty"* would filter the claim table and leave the line or denial
table whole — a shrinking denominator against a full numerator, which reads as a
plausible number and is wrong.

So `DimPayer`, `DimFacility`, `DimProvider` and `DimPatient` each hold a direct
relationship to **all six** facts. That costs four integers per row. The alternative
costs a number nobody can trust, and `verify_semantic_model.py` now fails the build if
any conformed dimension stops reaching any fact.

### 2. There is deliberately **no** relationship between the facts

With the dimensions conformed, a header-to-line relationship would give the engine two
paths from `DimPayer` to the line table. `ClaimID` is carried on every fact as a
degenerate key for drill-through instead.

The corollary is a rule the measures obey without exception: **money is defined once.**
The claim header owns billed, allowed and paid. `FactClaimLine[ChargeAmount]` sums to
the header's billed amount and is used for mix and volume only. `FactPayment` holds the
same cash as `FactClaim[PaidAmount]`, seen on the payment date instead of the service
date. No measure ever adds two of them together.

### 3. Four dates, one fact table

A claim has four dates and they answer different questions:

| Date | Answers |
|---|---|
| **Service** (active) | When was the care delivered? Clinical volume. |
| Submission | When did the payer's clock start? Everything about the receivable. |
| Adjudication | When did the payer decide? Denial workload. |
| Resolution | When did it become cash or a denial? Cash questions. |

Rather than four copies of the fact, there are four relationships — one active, three
inactive — and the **Date Basis** calculation group switches between them with
`USERELATIONSHIP`. One table, four clocks.

## The calculation groups, and why their precedence matters

| Group | Precedence | Items |
|---|---:|---|
| **Date Basis** | 10 | Service date · Submission date · Adjudication date · Resolution date |
| **Time Comparison** | 20 | Selected period · Prior month · Month over month · Month over month % · Prior year · Trailing 12 months |

Time Comparison has the **higher** precedence, so it wraps Date Basis. That ordering is
the whole point: *"prior month on submission date"* has to mean **the month before,
measured on submission** — not *"the submission-date version of last month's service
figure"*, which is not a question anybody asks. `verify_semantic_model.py` checks the
two precedences and the ordering between them, because a silent swap would change every
comparison on every page and break nothing visible.

## The stock, and the trick that makes it safe

`FactARSnapshot` is a **stock**: open AR at a month end. A stock must never be summed
across months — adding January's receivable to February's counts the same unpaid claim
twice, which is the easiest way to be confidently wrong about a revenue cycle.

Every AR measure therefore goes through one hidden measure, `[Snapshot Month End]`:

```dax
VAR AsOfEnd = EOMONTH ( [As-Of Date], 0 )
VAR LastInContext = MAX ( DimDate[MonthEnd] )
VAR FirstInContext = MIN ( DimDate[MonthEnd] )
RETURN
    IF ( FirstInContext <= AsOfEnd, MIN ( LastInContext, AsOfEnd ) )
```

It takes the last month end in context, caps it at the as-of month, and returns BLANK
when the whole selection is in the future. Selecting January to August gives the
receivable **at the end of August**. Selecting a month the file does not reach gives
nothing, rather than drawing today's number flat into next year.

`FactARMovement` is the matching **flow**, and it sums across months correctly. The two
close an identity in every one of the 47 months:

```
AR(m) = AR(m−1) + submitted − collected − patient responsibility − denied
```

`[AR Roll-Forward Check]` returns that difference, anchored to the snapshot month so it
is zero at *every* grain and not only inside a monthly visual.

## The identity the model exists to protect

Every dollar the payer allowed sits in exactly one of four buckets, on every row:

```
AllowedAmount = PaidAmount + PatientResponsibility + OpenARAmount + DeniedAmount
```

Because it holds row by row, it holds at every level of every aggregation, under any
slice, with or without row-level security. `[Allowed Identity Gap]` publishes the
difference — **$0.00** — on the method page, so the claim is visible rather than merely
asserted.

Measured live: allowed **$72,713,182.33** = collected **$46,414,738.26** + patient
responsibility **$3,768,666.11** + open AR **$16,830,999.94** + denied
**$5,698,778.02**. Open AR read independently from the snapshot agrees to the cent.

## Row-level security — `Facility Access`

Dynamic, from `USERPRINCIPALNAME()`, against the 82-row `SecurityUserAccess` table.
`ALL` is kept as a literal, so the expression is one comparison rather than a 30-row
scan per user. An unmapped user sees nothing, which is the secure default.

Four tables are filtered, and each for a reason:

| Table | Why |
|---|---|
| `DimFacility` | The scope itself. Filtering it restricts every fact. |
| `DimProvider` | Provider and facility are **one hierarchy** — every provider bills only at their own facility. Filtering only the facility would leave all 500 provider names in a slicer with nothing behind them. |
| `ProviderChance` | It is **disconnected**, so no relationship carries the facility filter to it. Left alone, a facility manager would read the whole group's provider distribution. |
| `SecurityUserAccess` | A user may see only their own mapping row. |

**`DimPatient` is deliberately not filtered**, and the verifier asserts that it isn't. A
patient may be treated at more than one facility, so a patient-level filter would be
wrong in both directions — and it is unnecessary, because the facility filter already
restricts every claim a scoped user can reach.

## The three disconnected tables

They are disconnected on purpose, and the reason is the same in each case: they are
statements **about** the claims, not slices **of** them, so letting a filter reach them
would silently change what they mean.

| Table | What it holds | Why a filter would break it |
|---|---|---|
| `DenialSignal` | A chi-square test of every candidate dimension against the denied flag, with Cramér's V | Filtering it by payer would be meaningless — payer is one of the things being tested |
| `ProviderChance` | Each provider's denial rate next to the binomial standard error for that provider's claim count | The group rate it compares against is computed over all claims; filtering would move the baseline without saying so |
| `DataQualityMetric` | The Phase 1 findings, recomputed on every refresh | A finding is a fact about the whole file |

`ProviderChance` is the exception that proves the rule: because it *is* disconnected, it
had to be given an explicit RLS filter of its own.

### Three measures worth reading

**`[Days in AR]`** — the receivable expressed as days of submitted business. The window
is **capped at the last submission date**, because claims stop being submitted on
7 September 2026 while cash goes on arriving until 3 November. Uncapped, it divides a
full receivable by seven days of business and reports a DSO of about **8,700 days**.
Capped, it reads 320 — which is still enormous, and is the honest number.

**`[Net Collection Rate %]`** — cash over allowed on **resolved claims only**, because a
revenue-cycle team is not accountable for claims the payer has not answered yet. It
reads 83.1%. `[Net Collection Rate (All Claims) %]` reads 63.8% and sits beside it,
because that is what the organisation has actually banked. Neither is allowed to be
quoted alone.

**`[Denial Recovery Rate %]`** — zero, by construction. It exists precisely so the page
can say that appeal yield is unmeasurable in this source, rather than leaving a reader
to assume it was overlooked.

## What the model refuses to do

| Not built | Why |
|---|---|
| A denial-risk model | Every candidate feature is inside \|r\| < 0.01. It would fit noise, and then be used to hold up claims that were never at risk |
| A provider denial league table | 23 of 500 providers sit beyond two standard errors; a normal distribution predicts 22.75. The distribution is published against its chance baseline instead |
| Appeal yield, overturn rate, recovery days | No denied claim in this file was ever paid, whatever its status says |
| Payer contract ranking | Every payer allows 73.4–73.6% of billed, including Self Pay, where no contract exists |
| Procedure revenue or case-mix | The charge is drawn independently of the CPT code — every code averages within $6 of every other |
| A growth narrative | Volume varies 3.3% month to month across 44 months. There is no trend to report |
